## Symptom

An article draft credits a correspondent with a sharp line from a public comment thread: "as they put
it, ...". A check confirms that every credited quote really appears in that person's comments. The
draft is ready to publish.

## What the instrument said

| the check | the result |
|---|---|
| does each credited quote appear in the credited person's text? | yes, all of them |

Green, and correct as far as it went.

## What was actually true

**The line was ours.** We had written it in an earlier comment on the same thread; the correspondent
had quoted it back while replying. So it did appear in their text, and they did not say it. Published,
the draft would have praised our own sentence under someone else's name.

The check answered "does this string occur in their words?" The question that attribution asks is
"who wrote this string FIRST?", and a quote-back makes those two come apart exactly when the line is
good enough to repeat.

## The rival, and the discriminator

**RIVAL:** both authors arrived at the same phrasing independently, so crediting either is fair.

**DISCRIMINATOR:** order the whole thread by time and find the EARLIEST occurrence of the quote. An
independent coinage appears first in their comment. A quote-back appears first in yours, and theirs
sits in a reply beneath it. A guard that does exactly this caught 4 of 4 credited quotes in the old
draft and 0 in the corrected one.

## The one-line check

Before crediting a quote, **find its earliest occurrence in the thread, not merely an occurrence**, and
credit whoever wrote that. Quote verbatim, because a paraphrase in quote marks cannot be checked at all.

```
# walk the thread oldest-first; the first author to contain the quote is its author
```

## The tell

**You are writing "as X put it", and the line sounds like something you would say.** It may be.
